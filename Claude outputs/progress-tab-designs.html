Progress tab — three easier-to-read designs

The problem with the first version: it showed every number at once and left you to work out what it meant. These three each lead with a plain-English answer ("Am I getting stronger? Am I missing anything?"), show one idea per section, use words and colours instead of raw numbers where possible (Improving / Holding / Dropping), and keep the detailed charts one tap away on the exercise screen. Same made-up data in all three.

A"Your month in a sentence"

Reads top to bottom like a short report.

  • Big headline answers the main question first
  • 3 insights with icons: best gain, PRs, what needs attention
  • Lifts list says Improving / Holding / Dropping instead of numbers
  • Muscles as simple ✓ / ! chips

BMuscle groups first

Start from the body, not from data.

  • 6 big tiles — Chest, Back, Shoulders, Arms, Legs, Core
  • Each tile: a coloured dot + one status line ("Getting stronger", "Trained less this week")
  • Tap a tile → only that muscle's exercises (shown: Legs)
  • Easiest for new users; no charts until you drill in

CThree questions, one at a time

A switch at the top: Strength · Consistency · Balance. Tap to try.

  • Each view answers one question with one sentence + one simple chart
  • Nothing else competes for attention
  • Good once users know what they want to check
How the words are decided (same in all three): an exercise is Improving if your top set over the last 4 sessions beats the 4 before, Holding if it's within a small step (2.5 kg / 1 rep), Dropping if it's below. A muscle is "behind" when this week's sets are under 60% of your usual week. Every row and tile opens the detailed exercise analytics screen you already have.
